Examples

Let us consider a complete 3 × 3 board and see how the rook numbers are obtained.

Example 1: Placing One Rook

A 3 × 3 board contains 9 squares. A single rook can be placed on any one of these squares.

♜

The rook can be placed in any of the 9 squares.

r1 = 9

Example 2: Placing Two Rooks

Two rooks must not be in the same row or the same column. Such rooks are called non-attacking rooks.

Now we count all possible valid placements.

Choose 2 different rows → 3 ways
Choose 2 different columns → 3 ways
Arrange the 2 rooks → 2 ways

r2 = 3 × 3 × 2 = 18

Therefore, there are 18 ways to place two non-attacking rooks.

Example 3: Placing Three Rooks

For three rooks, each rook must be placed in a different row and a different column.

One possible arrangement is shown above. Since no two rooks share a row or column, all three rooks are non-attacking.

The first rook has 3 choices, the second has 2 remaining choices, and the third has 1 remaining choice.

r3 = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6

Therefore, there are 6 ways to place three non-attacking rooks.

Complete Rook Polynomial

There is one way to place zero rooks: place no rooks on the board.

r0 = 1

Therefore, for the complete 3 × 3 board:

r0 = 1,   r1 = 9,   r2 = 18,   r3 = 6

Hence, the rook polynomial is:

RB(x) = 1 + 9x + 18x2 + 6x3

Example: Complete 2 × 2 Board

A complete 2 × 2 board has 4 available squares, so one rook has 4 possible placements. With two rooks, they must occupy different rows and columns; the only possibilities are the two diagonals.

r0 = 1,   r1 = 4,   r2 = 2
RB(x) = 1 + 4x + 2x2
